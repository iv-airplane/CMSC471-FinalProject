City in Motion

Visualizing taxi pickups and urban flow in New York City

New York City’s transportation system is constantly in motion, shaped by where people go, when they travel, and how they choose to get around. This project uses NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission (TLC) data to explore taxi and ride-hailing activity across the city. Through interactive visualizations, explore where trips are concentrated, how demand shifts, and how transportation modes have evolved over time.

Dataset

This project uses trip data from the NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission (TLC Trip Record Data) . The dataset contains detailed records of taxi and for-hire vehicle trips, including pickup time, location, and vehicle type, and spans millions of trips each month.

For our analysis, we focus on a few key fields: taxi zone ID, borough, pickup date and hour, vehicle type, and total trip count.

The main vehicle types in the dataset are:

Yellow Taxis
Traditional taxis, mostly in Manhattan and at airports
Green Taxis
Traditional taxis, mostly in the outer boroughs
For-Hire Vehicles (FHV)
Pre-booked car services like limousines and black cars
High Volume FHV (HVFHV)
App-based services like Uber and Lyft

Choropleth Map: Where do taxi trips happen?

This map shows taxi and for-hire vehicle pickups across NYC taxi zones for a single day. Each zone is colored based on the number of trips during a selected hour, with darker colors indicating higher activity. Use the slider or play button to move through the day and switch between vehicle types to compare patterns.

Heat Map: When do taxi trips happen?

This heatmap shows trip volume by hour of day and day of week. Each cell represents a specific time period, with darker colors indicating higher activity. Use the dropdown to compare patterns across vehicle types.

Market Share Chart: How have taxi trips evolved over time?

This chart shows how the share of trips by vehicle type has changed over time. The stacked bars display the proportion of total trips each year, while the donut chart highlights the breakdown for a selected year.

Implementation Details

Data Processing

The TLC dataset is very large, with millions of trips recorded each month, so we first needed to process and aggregate the data before using it in our visualizations. We used DuckDB to efficiently query the raw trip-level data and generate smaller, analysis-ready datasets.

From the original data, we created several aggregated datasets:

  • Trips by taxi zone, hour, and date (for the chloropleth map)
  • Trips by day of week and hour (for the heatmap)
  • Trips by year and vehicle type (for market share trends)

One challenge we encountered was that before 2019, high-volume FHV (HVFHV) trips were not tracked separately and were grouped with other FHV trips. As ride-hailing services became more common, the data began separating these categories. To make comparisons consistent over time, we manually split FHV and HVFHV during preprocessing.

To support the spatial visualization, we used NYC taxi zone shapefiles and converted them into GeoJSON using Mapshaper so they could be rendered in the browser.

Visualization

The visualizations are built using D3 and Vega-Lite. We use a taxi zone choropleth map to show how trip activity varies across space, a heatmap to highlight patterns across time, and a stacked bar chart with a donut chart to show changes in market share over time.

We chose a choropleth map to represent spatial patterns because it allows for easy comparison across zones, and a heatmap to show dense temporal patterns without clutter. The market share visualization uses normalized stacked bars to emphasize proportions rather than absolute values.

Interactive elements such as sliders, dropdowns, and tooltips allow users to explore different time periods and vehicle types, making it easier to compare patterns and understand how transportation activity changes across the city.
